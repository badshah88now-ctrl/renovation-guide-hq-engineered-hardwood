Engineered Hardwood Flooring Cost in 2026: Installed Prices and What to Expect

Engineered hardwood gives you a real wood surface over a layered plywood core, which makes it more stable than solid wood in rooms where humidity changes through the seasons. It looks and feels like solid hardwood underfoot, and the thicker veneers can even be sanded and refinished. The price depends mostly on veneer thickness, plank width, and the installation method.

According to Renovation Guide HQ's engineered hardwood flooring cost guide, engineered hardwood costs $7 to $20 per square foot installed in 2026, with a national average of $4,750 for a typical project, based on Angi data updated in October 2026. Materials run $3 to $16 per square foot, and professional labor adds $3 to $10. A 500-square-foot room usually totals $3,500 to $10,000, and most installs finish in 2 to 4 days.

Budget floors with a thin veneer and domestic oak, installed floating, run $7 to $10 per square foot, and a 500-square-foot job totals about $2,000 to $5,000. Mid-range floors with a 2 to 3 mm veneer, wide planks, and glue-down or nail-down installation run $10 to $14 per square foot, or about $5,000 to $9,000 for the same room. Premium European oak with a 3 to 6 mm veneer runs $14 to $20 per square foot, or about $8,000 to $16,000.

The veneer thickness is the detail that matters most for the long term. A thin 1 to 2 mm veneer cannot be sanded later, so it suits rentals and short-horizon plans. A 2 to 3 mm veneer holds one refinish in reserve and can realistically last 30 years or more with care. A 3 to 6 mm veneer allows two or more refinishes and behaves much like solid wood for decades.

Pattern layouts cost roughly double the standard labor, and smaller rooms cost more per square foot because the crew still has a minimum day rate. Get the subfloor checked and leveled first, confirm whether the manufacturer allows installation over your existing floor, and ask the installer to confirm the adhesive and moisture barrier in writing.
